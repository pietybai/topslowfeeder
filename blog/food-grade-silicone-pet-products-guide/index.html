Food Grade Silicone Pet Products: Safety, Benefits & Buying Guide

What the certification actually means, which standards matter, and how to vet a supplier

Published: November 2026 · By Marvin Bai, Top Slow Feeder

When pet owners see "food grade silicone" on a product listing, many assume it means safe. For buyers sourcing in volume, that assumption needs to be grounded in documented facts — certifications, third-party test reports, and supplier transparency. This guide covers what food grade silicone actually means, why it matters specifically for pet products, and what to verify before placing a wholesale order.

What Is Food Grade Silicone?

Silicone is a synthetic polymer made from silicon — a naturally occurring element — bonded with oxygen, carbon, and hydrogen. Food grade silicone specifically refers to silicone that has been independently tested to confirm it does not leach harmful substances when in contact with food or liquids.

Key material characteristics:

  • Chemically inert: does not react with food, beverages, or pet saliva
  • Temperature stable: safe from −40°C to 230°C without degrading
  • Non-porous: resists bacterial growth between uses
  • BPA-free and phthalate-free
  • No odor or taste transfer to food

The phrase "food grade" is not a self-declared marketing term. It must be substantiated by recognized regulatory testing standards. A supplier who cannot produce a test report is making an unverified claim.

Regulatory Standards for Food Grade Silicone

The main certifications relevant to pet products sold in Western markets are:

FDA (United States)

Under 21 CFR 177.2600, silicone used in food contact applications must meet specific compositional and extractable limits. Products exported to the US should be tested against FDA standards. For pet products, this applies because the silicone is in direct contact with food placed on or in the mat.

LFGB (European Union / Germany)

The German Food and Commodity Items Act (LFGB) is widely used as the benchmark for food contact materials across Europe. LFGB testing is considered more stringent than the FDA equivalent in several areas and is the preferred certification for EU and UK retail buyers. If you are selling into European markets, request LFGB compliance documentation specifically.

REACH (EU)

The EU's REACH regulation ensures that no substances of very high concern (SVHC) are present in the product. REACH compliance is a baseline requirement for any product sold into EU distribution channels.

For pet products — slow feeder mats, lick mats, and bowls — all three standards are relevant because the product is in regular contact with food and animal saliva, is routinely dishwashed at high temperatures, and may be chewed or licked directly.

Why Food Grade Silicone Is Right for Pet Products

Pet owners are applying the same safety standards to pet products that they apply to items used for children. Products that meet a higher bar on safety claims generate stronger customer trust, fewer compliance-related returns, and better positioning in premium retail channels.

For wholesale buyers and private label brands, food grade certification has a direct commercial effect:

  • Retail channel access: major pet specialty retailers increasingly require food contact certification for pet feeding accessories
  • Amazon listing protection: certified products are less likely to be flagged during product compliance reviews
  • Customer review quality: verified safe materials are consistently mentioned positively in organic reviews
  • Lower return exposure: non-toxic, odor-free products produce fewer complaints about material quality

Buying Guide: What to Check When Sourcing Silicone Pet Products

When evaluating a silicone pet products supplier, use the following checklist before placing a bulk order:

1. Request Third-Party Test Reports, Not Just Claims

A supplier should provide actual lab test reports from an accredited body — SGS, Intertek, Bureau Veritas, or TÜV — showing FDA or LFGB compliance. A verbal "food grade" assurance or an internal certificate is not sufficient for buyers selling in regulated retail channels.

2. Confirm BPA-Free and Phthalate-Free in Writing

These should be explicitly stated in the test report or the product specification sheet. Do not accept a general "non-toxic" claim as a substitute for documented absence of specific substances.

3. Check Production Batch Consistency

Material quality can shift between production runs if a supplier changes material sources. Ask whether the supplier retests certifications periodically, and whether the test reports on file reflect the current production batch.

4. Evaluate Structural Integrity

Food grade silicone used for pet feeding products should be firm enough to maintain shape under a pet's weight on a suction-mounted mat. If a sample folds or collapses easily, it may indicate a lower-grade silicone compound that will not hold up to daily use.

5. Ask About Shelf Life and Storage Conditions

Food grade silicone pet products typically carry a shelf life of 2–5 years when stored away from direct sunlight and extreme heat. This matters for wholesale buyers managing FBA or bonded warehouse inventory over extended periods.
